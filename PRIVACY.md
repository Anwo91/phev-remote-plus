# PHEV Link — privacy policy

*Last updated: 30 October 2026 (a trip one of your phones records on its own OBD adapter goes to your car hub and on to your other phones, and the car hub's push tells your phones when it has saved a trip; earlier: your car hub also pushes the car's state after each change, such as plugged in or charging finished, encrypted like the alerts, so your widget is up to date without your phone asking; your phone tells your car hub which kinds of alert you have ticked, so it pushes only those; earlier: smart charging at the places you choose, priced for that place; resets of learned values are shared between your devices; crash reports through Firebase Crashlytics, with the last log lines cleaned, and a switch to turn them off; instant alerts come by push, encrypted end to end, through Firebase Cloud Messaging and our push relay; where the car is, on the Car tab; earlier: your settings can be kept in Android's own backup, and saved to or restored from a file; the monthly costs can be saved as a spreadsheet; departures can come from your calendar; places you save, and the car arriving at and leaving them)*

PHEV Link is an app for the Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV (2014–2017), with an optional "bridge" (a small computer such as a
Raspberry Pi) that you run at home. It is made by Anwo91, a sole proprietorship in Norway ("we").

**In short:** there is no PHEV Link account and no PHEV Link server that keeps anything. What the app learns about your
car, your trips and your charging stays on your phone and on your own bridge. The app only talks to the outside services
listed below, for the reasons given. The one piece of ours on the internet is a small push relay for instant alerts,
which passes on alerts it can't read (they are encrypted for your phones) and keeps nothing. When the app crashes, a
crash report comes to us through Google's Firebase Crashlytics, with places, names and addresses taken out; you can
switch that off. The Google Play version also shows ads from Google until you buy PHEV Link Plus.

## What the app uses, and where it goes

| Precise location, also in the background (optional, off by default) | To measure trips (route and distance) for the electric range | Stays on your phone. The place, rounded to about 1 km, is shared with your own bridge and paired phones (for departures they start) and sent to MET Norway (api.met.no) to get the forecast; the last forecast, with the place it is for, is kept on your phone. There is no setting to type a place in. |
| The electric range the car hub learned (car hub mode only) | Home Assistant's range and the weekly summary on your bridge | The car hub's range per full battery (now and at 20 °C, the temperature, how many drives it is from) is kept with the shared data on the car hub and copied to your bridge at home. Never to us. |

Never to us. |
| When you reset a learned value (Settings → Learned values) | So every phone, the car hub and the bridge learn that value again from the same moment | Which value (such as the electric range or the fuel correction) and the time of its last reset, kept on your phone and, with a bridge, with the shared data on your bridge or car hub so your other phones follow it. Nothing is deleted from History. Never to us. |
| Where the car is, rounded to about 1 km (car hub mode only) | The weather forecast where the car is, for smart climate (departures) and the range estimate | The car hub's last known location, rounded to about 1 km, is passed to your own paired phones over the car hub's bridge, and sent to MET Norway (api.met.no) for the forecast. Never to us. Without it, the saved location is used. |
| Where the car is, exactly (the Car tab's "Where the car is") | So you can see where the car is parked or driving, and open that place in your maps app | With a car hub: when your phone shows the Car tab (at most once a minute) or you pull it down to refresh, it asks the car hub, which reads its own location (a fresh fix when the one it knows is more than 2 minutes old) and sends it, with when and how precise, to the asking phone only, over the car hub's bridge (Tailscale or your home Wi-Fi). It isn't kept on the car hub or in the shared data; your phone keeps the last answer, to show it when the car hub can't be reached (not in the settings backup). Without a car hub, the card shows where your newest trip with a route ended (already on your phone). The map around it comes from OpenStreetMap's tile servers, as for the route map. "Open in maps" hands the place to the maps app you choose on your phone, whose own privacy policy applies. The log says where a position came from, how precise and how old it is, never the coordinates. Never to us. |
| The area around a trip's route, a place or the car | To draw the route map, a place's map and the map of where the car is | Map pictures are fetched from OpenStreetMap's tile servers (tile.openstreetmap.org), which see which map area is shown and your IP address. |
| Nearby Wi-Fi networks (car hub mode only, optional) | On a spare phone left in the car: to tell when the car is at home (your home Wi-Fi in sight), so charges are kept and smart charging runs only there | Stays on that phone. Android counts seeing Wi-Fi networks as location, so it asks for location access "all the time". |
| The car hub's bridge (car hub mode only) | Your own phone reaches the car through the spare phone in the car; the car hub also tells it which of its permissions and settings are OK (Bluetooth, location, notifications, battery, background data, alarms, installing updates, motion sensor — only allowed or not — and whether its battery is kept full on power or stops at a charging limit, worked out from its own battery level and charging state), so you can see from your own phone what to fix. It also tells its own battery level and whether it is charging; your phone keeps the last one, to say so if the car hub stops answering | Only between your own devices: over Tailscale (a private network you set up with your own Tailscale account) or your home Wi-Fi. The car hub never answers on mobile data. |
| Built-in Tailscale (car hub mode, and the owner's phone when its bridge is a car hub) | The spare phone in the car joins your own Tailscale network (your tailnet) so your phones and your bridge reach it; your own phone joins it too, inside the app, to reach the car hub from anywhere — only PHEV Link's connections to the car hub go through it (no VPN), and it stops when unused | Connects to Tailscale's coordination and relay servers like any Tailscale device, under your own Tailscale account and Tailscale's privacy policy. No logs are sent to Tailscale. The names and Tailscale addresses of the other devices in your tailnet are shown on the car hub and passed to your own paired phone (to tell why it can't reach the car hub); they stay on your devices. |
| Instant alerts (owner's phone with a car hub or bridge, on by default, Settings → Notifications) | So your car hub's (or bridge's) alerts reach your phone within seconds with the app closed | Google's Firebase gives the app an installation id and a push token (an address for push messages to this app on this phone); your phone gives the token to your car hub (or bridge) only, with which kinds of alert you have ticked at home and away (so it sends only those). When the car hub raises an alert, it encrypts it, with the car's battery level, charging, plug, lock, doors, lights, ignition and climate at that moment, with a key only your paired phones have, and sends it through our push relay (on Cloudflare Workers) and Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) to your phone. After a change (plugged in or out, charging started or stopped, climate on or off, locked or unlocked, the car switched off, or the battery level 5 points on), it sends that same car state the same way, so your widget is up to date without your phone asking; when it has saved a trip, that push also says when the trip ended, and your phone then fetches the trip from the car hub. Neither we, Cloudflare nor Google can read it: they see that a message of a few kilobytes went to that token, and when, and Cloudflare and Google see the car hub's IP address. The relay keeps nothing (its logs are off). Firebase is used only for this and for crash reports (below): no Firebase Analytics, no advertising id. Google's privacy information for Firebase: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy. Switching instant alerts off removes the token from the car hub and deletes it on the phone. |
| Drives recorded by the car hub (car hub mode only) | The car hub connects to the OBD adapter when the car is switched on and records the drive (distance, speed, engine and battery use; and the route with the car hub's GPS, on by default on a car hub when location "all the time" is allowed there; on other phones only with "Route with GPS" on), so every drive shows in History → Trips on your phones, whoever drove. The car hub also watches its own motion sensor (Android's "significant motion", no permission needed) and its accelerometer (how much the phone is shaken, read on the car hub only, no permission needed): moving counts as a sign the car may be on, and makes it look at the car. Only the largest movement per hour and each look go into its log | Kept on the car hub and passed to your own paired phones over the car hub's bridge (Tailscale or your home Wi-Fi). A drive one of your phones records on its own OBD adapter (with its route, and the phone's name as who recorded it) is sent to the car hub when it ends and passed on the same way, so every phone has the same trips; when the car hub recorded the same drive, the two become one trip. When a trip is saved, the car hub tells your phones with the same sealed push as the car's state (only that a trip ended and when), and they fetch it from the car hub. Never to us. |
| Camera (optional) | Only to scan the code of "Add another phone" (Settings → Car hub → Join a car hub), asked when you tap Scan | The picture stays on the phone and isn't kept; only the code's text is used. |
| Another person's phone (car hub mode, optional) | "Add another phone" lets someone else use the car from their own phone: you share just the car hub with their own Tailscale account in Tailscale, and give them a one-time code | The code works once, for a day, and is kept on the car hub only as a hash. Sharing is done in Tailscale under your account; they see only the car hub. |
| Car health over time (car hub mode, or a phone with its own OBD adapter) | 12 V battery voltage, drive battery health, capacity, cell voltages and temperatures, the fault codes found, with when, and when the car was last switched off — so you can see how the car is doing over months and get a warning when the 12 V battery is low or a new fault code appears | Kept on the phone that reads the adapter (at most the last 30 looks with the car on, 48 parked 12 V readings, 40 fault codes and 36 months). In car hub mode also passed to your own paired phones and your bridge at home with the shared data, and to your Home Assistant if you set that up. Never to us. |
| OBD readings through the car hub (car hub mode only, optional) | Your own phone reads the car's diagnostics (live values, fault codes, tests) through the OBD adapter connected to the car hub — or, while the car hub is connected to the car itself, it shows the car hub's live values and has the car hub read or clear the fault codes. Your phone can also list the Bluetooth devices paired on or found near the car hub (names and addresses) to choose its adapter; a PIN you give for it is kept on the car hub to pair with it. While the dashboard is open, new values are sent about once a second (more mobile data than the OBD tab otherwise uses). While your phone borrows the car hub's adapter (live tests, terminal), it sends its live readings back to the car hub every few seconds, so the car hub keeps recording the drive | Passed straight between your own two phones, the same way as the car hub's bridge, with the bridge's key. Nothing more is kept on the car hub. |
| Bluetooth devices | To connect to an OBD adapter, and to notice when your phone joins the car's Bluetooth | Stays on your phone. |
| Your electricity price area | Charging costs and smart charging | Your bridge fetches the day's prices for that area from hvakosterstrommen.no (Norway) or elprisetjustnu.se (Sweden). Only the area and the date are sent. |
| The app's own log | To find out what went wrong | Stays on your phone: kept in the app's private storage (at most about 8 MB, the oldest lines dropped first) so it survives restarts and crashes. It only leaves the phone if you share it yourself (Settings → Help and support), and, with crash reports on, its last lines go with a crash report, cleaned first (next line). The car hub's log can be fetched by your own paired phone (Settings → Car hub → Get its log), over the car hub's bridge, to share it from there. |
| Crash reports (both versions, on by default; Settings → About → Send crash reports, for each phone) | So we learn of crashes and freezes on every phone, not only ours, and can fix them | When the app crashes or freezes (stops responding), Google's Firebase Crashlytics sends a report to our Firebase project: what went wrong and where in the app's code, the phone's make and model, the Android version, the app version, free memory and storage, whether the phone is rooted, when, a random installation id made by Firebase (not linked to you), which version of the app (GitHub or Google Play), whether the phone is a car hub or in demo mode, and the app's last log lines (at most 64 kB). Nothing is sent anywhere. Never to us. |

## Google Play version: purchases and ads

**Purchases.** PHEV Link Plus and tips are bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we only learn from
Google Play whether Plus has been bought (no name, address or payment details). See Google's privacy policy:
https://policies.google.com/privacy

**Ads.** Until PHEV Link Plus is bought, the Google Play version shows small ads from Google AdMob. To show ads, measure
them and prevent fraud, Google's ads software on your phone collects information such as your device's advertising ID,
your IP address and the approximate location derived from it, and how ads are shown and tapped. In the EEA, the UK and
Switzerland, Google asks for your consent first (personalised ads, or not), and you can change your answer at any time
under Settings → PHEV Link Plus → Ad privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's
settings. How Google uses this information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

With PHEV Link Plus there are no ads, and the ads software is not started.

## How long it is kept

Everything the app keeps is on your phone (and on your bridge, if you have one) until you delete it in the app, clear the
app's data or uninstall it. The only thing that comes to us is crash reports, which Google's Firebase Crashlytics keeps
for us for 90 days. The push relay passes encrypted alerts on and keeps nothing.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of personal data about you. The only data from your phone we
hold is crash reports, for 90 days, under a random installation id we can't link to you (switch crash reports off to
stop them); for data collected by Google for ads and purchases, see Google's privacy policy and
https://myaccount.google.com. You can also complain to the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet).

## Children

PHEV Link is made for car owners and is not directed at children.
